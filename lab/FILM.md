# Films: the points to remember

The film director's checklist (`.claude/agents/film-director.md`) for the game's short films: 15–45 seconds, on a
phone held upright or sideways, played in the game's own engine with its own people, skippable, never a wall of text.
Each point is short on purpose; its source is there for the why. Read it before writing a film, and check the film
against it before it is shown.

## 1. The story

- **Write the turn first, in one line.** _At the start ___, at the end ___._ A scene that does not change a value
  (here: power, ownership, pride) is a non-event; cut it. [McKee, _Do your scenes turn?_][mckee]
- **One logline before any shot.** The story's essence in its most economical telling, then build out. [Pixar's
  rules, #22][pixar]
- **Enter late, leave early.** Start in the middle of the deal, not at the door; leave the moment the turn has landed
  (plus the button, below). No arrivals, no greetings, no small talk. [Goldman via Scott Myers][late]
- **Someone must doubt.** A reward feels earned only against resistance: the one who underrated the player, a price
  that looked impossible. The film overturns it. (The reversal is the turn made visible.)
- **Suspense before surprise.** Show the thing before it pays off (the case is carried in, set down, its lid still
  shut) and let the audience wait a beat; a surprise is two seconds, an anticipation is the whole middle. [Hitchcock
  to Truffaut][hitch]; the reveal window is where the pleasure is. [PC Gamer on reveal design][reveal]
- **Rule of three, the third one breaks.** Two beats set a pattern, the third twists it. Use it once per film, not on
  every joke. [No Film School][three]
- **Call back to play.** The strongest joke is one the player is in on: a number, a word, a sound they know from the
  game (the manager's "20%"). A film made of the game's own facts also stays true when the game grows.
- **End on a button, then on the reward.** After the climax, one short comic tag; then the end card with what the
  player got, in the game's own pictures. Memory keeps the peak and the end: put the peak late and end high. [Peak-end
  in games, CHI 2016][peak]
- **No conflict? Use four beats.** For a cosy moment: introduce, develop, twist (a change of viewpoint), settle
  (kishōtenketsu, as Nintendo builds levels). [Wikipedia][kisho] · [Suzy Cube on Mario 3D Land][kisho2]
- **An animal's gag needs no words.** If a joke needs dialogue, drop it (Shaun the Sheep's rule): the herd acts with
  its body (a walk, a turn, its head up) and speaks in the game's own sounds (a cluck, a moo, a bleat, the snip of the
  shears); the people say the one or two lines. [Burton and Starzak on Shaun the Sheep][shaun]
- **The look into the lens is the animal's line.** After the gag, one held beat of the animal looking straight at us
  (Gromit, Oliver Hardy) shares the joke with the audience: Daisy after the kick, Dolly at the snip. [ACMI on Wallace &
  Gromit][gromit]
- **The build is one shot.** The crate lands, opens and becomes the tool, its pieces rising one after another with a
  click each; the building itself happens off screen, as a game's builder does it overnight. [Stardew Valley: the
  coop][coop]
- **Promise only what play gives.** The film shows what the act really does in the game; anything more (a new sign, a
  title) is a note for the game, not a lie in the film.

## 2. The words

- **A line is at most 38 characters, one idea, one breath.** At most two lines on screen, ~15 characters a second to
  read, never under 0.9 s or over 7 s. [IGDA GA-SIG top ten][gasig] · [Netflix timed text][netflix]
- **Every line earns its place** or is cut; visuals and play already say most of it. [10 tips for mobile game
  dialogue][mobiledlg]
- **Each character has a voice of their own** (word choice, rhythm, length) and **never says their subtext** (the
  boss never says "I feel small"; he offers to sell you his mug). [Dialogue don'ts][donts]
- **The player is the quiet one.** A silent hero acts; others talk. Give the player one line at most, the last
  laugh, and it lands because it is the only one.
- **Always show who speaks**: the bubble points to the speaker, the speaker's name in their colour; the film works
  with the sound off. [Xbox accessibility guideline 104][xag]
- **Every line is heard in words, in clear English, in its speaker's voice.** The device's own speech voices read it
  (speechSynthesis: on every phone, nothing downloaded or recorded, a changed line costs nothing); a character is a
  voice (a man's or a woman's, a different one each where the device has several), a pitch and a pace, and the mood
  moves them: the boss quick and a little high, the big man slow and deep, the player low and level. A babble made from
  the text (Animalese) carried the mood but not the meaning — the user could not follow it (round 3) — so it is left
  only for a device with no English voice. Phones speak only after a touch: Sound, or any tap on the film.
  [SpeechSynthesis][speech] · [touch first on phones][speechios] · [Animalese][animalese]
- **Plain English.** School English, followed on first hearing: a subject and a verb ("You? Buy my shop?", not "With
  what?"), no idioms ("mint condition", "heavy one"), numbers as they are said ("fourteen ninety-nine"). Give a line
  the time its speaker takes to say it; a voice hurried past 1.2× is no longer clear. [Plain language][plain]
- **The words come up as they are said** (the voice's own word boundaries), and the speaker's head dips with the
  syllables: picture, voice and words agree, silent too. [boundary][boundary]

## 3. The camera

- **Say where, then go close.** One wide shot to place us; then mostly medium shots and close-ups, subject in the
  middle: on a phone, details in a wide shot are lost. [Mobile composition][mobile]
- **The angle is the power.** Low looks up at strength (or awe); high looks down at the small. Swap them as the power
  changes hands: the doubter from below at first, the player from below at the end. [StudioBinder, low angle][low]
- **The 180° rule.** Two people talking stay on their own sides of the screen: the camera keeps to one side of the
  line between them; a cut on the same subject moves at least 30°. [Continuity editing][cont]
- **Cut on action.** A movement begun in one shot finishes in the next (the case lifted, then landing); the cut
  vanishes and the film keeps its speed. [Continuity editing][cont]
- **A move means something.** Push in: this matters. Pull out: reveal. Crane up: triumph. Shake: impact. Hold
  still: comedy (wide, square, deadpan frames read as jokes). [Edgar Wright's visual comedy][wright]
- **The laugh is often in the reaction**, but one reaction shot per joke; more is a laugh track. [Directing a comedy
  short][reaction]
- **Slow motion turns a walk into a procession.** Once per film, for the hero. [Reservoir Dogs' walk][walk]
- **Frame subjects, never coordinates**, so a shot works upright and sideways, and keeps working when the place or the
  people change.
- **Upright, stage in depth.** Two people side by side on a narrow screen are small; over a shoulder or at three
  quarters they fill it. Name the one the shot favours first.
- **Medium beats close in this game's look.** The people engine's faces are simple and do not move; a medium shot
  lets the body act (a laugh, arms up, a snap). Keep close-ups for things (a screen, the case) and for one line.
- **Film the side of the world that is the same for every player.** Across the lane is the player's own farm, which
  grows with play; a film never turns to it (`looksAtFarm`; `moments.mjs` warns), so the lab shows no farm that is not
  theirs and the film needs no keeping up. Around the set: the game's own sky, haze, lawn, lane and woods, never an
  empty colour. In the game the film plays in the live scene, so whatever does show is theirs.
- **For the last word, two side by side beat shot and reverse shot.** Both facing us, square, the shop behind: the
  one who answers without turning to look is the cool one, and no camera has to look back at the farm.
- **A fence is a set's too.** In the pen a fence section between the camera and the herd is left out like a wall, and
  so is one right at the lens (a post a hand away fills a side of the picture); the herd is filmed from the lane's side,
  so the camera looks at the woods, never back at the farm.
- **Small things that matter are drawn a little big.** Eggs on a nest's tray are a few pixels on a phone: the thing a
  shot is about may be half again its size (the coop's eggs), never what only dresses the set.
- **Walls are a set's, not a prison.** A wall between the camera and its subject is left out (wild walls); never one
  behind the subject, or the outside shows through.
- **Light the faces while they talk.** A key from in front, on the side the face turns to (short key: the far cheek
  lit, the near one in shade — cinema's usual), a rim from behind against the camera to part them from the set, and the
  set's own light stepped back so the faces are the brightest thing (the picture's edges darkened). Colours from the
  scene's sources: a desk lamp's warm key, a cold night edge; where a prop is the light (the case's gold) the key steps
  back. The rim only from medium in, close and narrow: in a full shot it lights the walls, not them. [Short key][short]
  · [a key and a rim per shot in game cinematics][cry]
- **A body acts at its joints.** A head nods at the foot of its neck: turned about its own middle, the neck slid out
  of the collar (round 3). When a body part leaves its place, the fix is the rig's (the people engine), for every film
  and the game. [Rigify: bones where the body bends][rig]

## 4. Rhythm

- **Shots of 1–4 s; a held beat (about half a second) before a punchline;** one frame can decide a joke. [Tony Zhou
  on editing comedy][zhou]
- **Anticipation before every big action**, exaggeration, one clear idea per shot (staging), follow-through. [Disney's
  12 principles][disney]
- **Things entering and leaving the frame, and a sound right on the cut,** are the cheapest jokes there are.
  [Edgar Wright][wright]

## 5. Sound and music

- **Music names the world, then turns with the story.** Change the cue at the turn; the contrast of two styles is
  itself a joke (muzak → a gangster beat).
- **Stingers punctuate**: a short phrase on the moment (a thud, a ka-ching, a choir). A win's sting sounds conclusive.
  [Win and lose stingers][sting]
- **Silence before the drop.** A rise, a short silence, then the hit makes a small reveal feel big. [PC Gamer on
  reveal design][reveal]
- **Every sting on a cut or an action** (the case lands, the lights come on): picture and sound hit together.
- **The music steps back under a voice** (about half), in one dip for lines close together, and comes back after.

## 6. Achievement

- **Celebrate the player's own act** and show the world changed by it: before and after in one place (the lights
  come on, the sign, the first shoppers).
- **Layer the feedback**: light, shake, particles, sound, a number that counts — together, once, at the peak.
  [Juice it or lose it][juice]
- **In-engine, so it is theirs**: the player in the clothes they wear now, the farm as it is. Pre-rendered films
  cannot show that. [In-engine cinematics][engine]
- **Emotion first.** Decide what the player should feel at each beat (doubt, suspense, triumph, a laugh); camera,
  light, sound and timing all serve it. [Blizzard on cinematics][blizzard]
- **The end card is progress, not a receipt.** The level bar fills from where it was with the XP the moment gave (a
  level crossed is a show of its own: the bar flashes, the badge pops), what is theirs now comes in one by one, and
  what it opens next is named — each part a beat after the last, within a second and a half, then two buttons. The
  user asked for it (round 4: "a sense of progress"). [Achievement pop-ups][xbox] · [show the next reward][next]

## 7. On a phone, in a game

- **Skippable from the first second**; skip lands on the end card, so the reward is never lost. Replay from a menu.
  [The problem with mandatory cutscenes][skip]
- **Works silent** (subtitles and visual stings carry it) and **with less motion** (cuts instead of shakes and whip
  pans).
- **Big words, thin bars**: subtitles at least 17 px on the game's paper; letterbox bars that never eat the picture
  upright; nothing under the notch or the home bar.
- **Small enough to play anywhere**: the game's own models and procedural sound, no video file, nothing to download.

## Before it is shown

1. The turn and the logline, one line each. 2. Read every line aloud with a stopwatch. 3. Look at every shot upright
and sideways (`node lab/films/moments.mjs <board> <dir> <seconds…>`), its motion (`node scripts/lab-film.mjs`), the
score's loudness (`moments.mjs <board> --levels`), and watch it silent and in slow motion. 4. Cut 10%. 5. Check the
beats above: who doubts, the callback, the held beat, the low angle that swaps, the sting on the cut, the button, the
end card.

## How a film is made here

A film is data (`src/film/format.ts`): its cast (the people engine's looks, and their voices: `src/film/voice.ts`,
read aloud by `src/film/speak.ts`), its set (the game's own rows), its shots (what they frame, from where, how big, at
what angle, how the camera moves), its lines, its moves and its cues. Its stage (`src/film/stage3d.ts`) plays any film
from that data on the game's own models (`src/models/person.ts`, `src/models/market.ts`, `src/models/goods.ts`), its
clock and sound (`src/film/run.ts`) on the game's own sound engine, framing each shot for the screen it is on and
leaving out the walls in the camera's way; the overlay and the end card are the game's (`src/ui/Film.tsx`). The game
plays it on its own canvas when its moment asks (`src/film/films.ts`, `StoryFilm.tsx`), the lab in a view of its own
(`lab/films/Player.tsx`). A new film is a new data file; a change to the game's people or places needs no change to
the films.

Why this and not a library: Remotion's Player would play a composition in the browser, but its licence asks companies
of four or more for a paid company licence, and it is made for videos, not for a game's own 3D scene. Theatre.js keys
animation in a studio (AGPL) and has had no release since 2024. A pre-rendered video cannot show the player's clothes
or follow the game's looks, and weighs megabytes on a phone. The game already has every piece: three.js and R3F for
the camera, the people engine for the cast, Motion for the overlay, a procedural sound engine.
